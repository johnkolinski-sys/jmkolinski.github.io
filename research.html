Research

Uncovering universal patterns in how cracks form and surfaces come into contact.

The mechanical behavior of soft materials is governed by their interfaces. Yet, despite remarkable advances in recent decades, critical questions about the mechanics of interfaces involving propagating stress singularities — cracks and contact lines — remain open. Propagating singularities present a unique challenge: large-scale forces cause sharp features to move, such as an advancing contact line or the tip of a growing crack. These phenomena share a common mathematical framework, but the material's response often becomes non-linear as it is pushed to its limits, and this non-linearity challenges the validity of existing theoretical models.

We confront these challenges by experimentally investigating the mechanical behavior of soft interfaces. Leveraging direct observation and cutting-edge imaging techniques, we gather crucial kinematic data. This enables us to unravel phenomena like symmetry breaking, identify the critical scales that clarify stress divergence at singular points, and understand how surface stresses modify the overall behavior of soft materials — bridging the gap between theoretical predictions and real-world material response.

How does a crack propagate in 3D?

The geometry of the crack front can profoundly influence toughness. Using direct imaging in 3D, we explore the geometry–toughness relationship in brittle solids. Our work shows that complexity in crack-front geometry can enhance the toughness of brittle materials, revealing hidden 3D structure within crack fronts that reflects the underlying energy balance.

Complexity of crack front geometry enhances toughness of brittle solids — Nature Physics (2024) →

How does a droplet splash? How does air mediate contact?

When a droplet approaches a solid, a thin film of air is trapped and must drain before contact forms. We study the onset of wetting beneath an impacting droplet on atomically smooth mica surfaces, resolving the characteristic rupture height of the mediating air film and the sequence of events by which contact is established.

Characteristic rupture height of the mediating air film — Phys. Rev. Fluids (2023) →

Experimental methods

Direct observation is at the heart of our approach. We develop and apply high-speed imaging, interferometry, and particle-tracking techniques — including the virtual frame technique for ultra-fast imaging with conventional cameras, and machine-learning-assisted interferometric imaging of liquid–solid interfaces — to measure deformation and interface kinematics with quantified error.
